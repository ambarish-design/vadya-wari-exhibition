01 / The encounter

Before a child learns the name of an instrument, they can meet its voice.

A Vādya Wāri visit is a shared listening space. Children notice shape, material and vibration; hear how sound travels; and encounter the people, places and practices that keep these instruments alive.

It is not a lecture and not a performance at a distance. It is an invitation to wonder: What is this made of? How does it speak? Whose hands have carried this music forward?

03 / For hosts

A visit belongs wherever children have room to be curious.

We work with each host to shape a visit around the children, the setting and the time available.

A

Schools

For classrooms seeking a living connection to art, sound, culture and inquiry.

B

Learning spaces

For community centres, libraries and after-school programmes where access matters deeply.

C

Public gatherings

For cultural spaces and neighbourhood events that want to make room for young listeners.

A good visit does not need a stage. A quiet corner, a circle of children and an openness to listen can be enough.

04 / Make access possible

A child’s first encounter with an instrument should not depend on their postcode.

Partnerships allow Vādya Wāri to reach learning spaces that may not have the budget, transport or time to arrange such an experience on their own.
